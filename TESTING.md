# Testing this on a fresh machine

Not part of the kit. This is for whoever is running the test.

## There are two different tests. Do them in this order.

**Test 1 — does it actually work?** You run it yourself, on a machine that has
never had Claude on it. You're checking the mechanics: does every step complete,
do the skills land, does the check at the end pass. Half an hour. Do this one first,
because if the install is broken you'll waste someone else's goodwill finding out.

**Test 2 — does it explain itself?** Someone who doesn't know how it works runs it,
and you watch without helping. You're checking comprehension: where do they pause,
what do they ask, where would they have given up. This is the test you cannot do
yourself, because you already know the answers.

Test 1 needs a clean machine. Test 2 needs a clean machine **and** a clean person —
so don't use the same laptop for both unless you reset it.

---

## Test 1 — you run it

On the clean machine:

1. AirDrop `Claude Kit.zip` across, unzip onto the Desktop.
2. Install the Claude desktop app from the links in `START-HERE.md`.
3. Sign in. Your own account is fine — your house rules and memory live on your
   own machine, not in the account, so it's still a clean start.
4. Follow `START-HERE.md` exactly as written. **Do not improvise.** If a step
   doesn't work, that's the finding — note it and try the next one.

Write down:

- Every step that didn't do what it said
- Anything you had to work out that the kit should have told you
- The nine-line check at the end, copied exactly
- How long the whole thing took

Then fix what you found before doing Test 2.

---

## Test 2 — someone else runs it

Same setup as above, on a machine that's still clean. You sit beside them with a
notes app open on your phone.

**Do not help.** This is the hard part and the only part that matters.

When they get stuck, write down where, and wait. Give it a full minute of them
looking confused before you say anything. That minute is the test.

**Let it fail.** A step that doesn't work is the most valuable thing you'll get all
day. Note exactly what they did and what happened.

**Only step in when they're properly blocked** — nothing on screen tells them what
to do next, and they've tried. Then note that you had to, and what you said.

**Don't explain in advance.** No "oh it'll probably ask you for X". If they need
that, the kit should have said it.

## What to write down

| | |
|---|---|
| Time started / finished | the real number, it'll be longer than you think |
| Every place they paused | and roughly how long |
| Every question they asked you | the question, word for word |
| Every time you helped | and what you said |
| Anything that errored | screenshot it |
| The nine-line check at the end | copy it out exactly |

Word for word matters on the questions. "What does connected mean?" and "Which
folder?" point at completely different problems.

## At the end, ask them four things

Ask before they've had time to reflect, and write the answers as they say them:

1. **What were you most confused by?**
2. **Was there a point you'd have given up if I wasn't here?**
3. **What do you reckon it actually does, now?** — tests whether the kit explained
   itself, or whether they're just following steps
4. **Would you open it again tomorrow on your own?**

Question 2 is the one that matters most. Everything else is fixable wording.

## Bring back

- The nine-line check output
- Your notes
- The four answers
- Screenshots of anything that broke

Paste all of it into Claude on your own machine and say *"this is the test run on a
fresh laptop — what do we fix?"*

## What a good test looks like

Not a clean run. A clean run means either the kit is finished or the test wasn't
honest, and the first one is unlikely.

A good test is three or four real stumbles, written down precisely enough to fix.
