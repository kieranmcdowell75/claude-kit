# Connecting your tools

This is where the value is. An afternoon here beats a hundred skills.

## The principle

You don't need a pre-written guide for each piece of software. Claude can work out
how to connect most things itself, as long as you point it at the problem properly
and stay nearby to sign in.

## What to say

Start a new session with the folder connected, and say one of these:

> "I want to connect my Xero. Check whether there's an official integration first,
> and if there isn't, find the best way in. Tell me what you need from me."

> "Walk me through connecting my business email."

> "We need to get at my booking software. There's no official integration as far as
> I know, but don't take my word for it — check. Then tell me the options."

That's it. Claude does the work. Your two jobs are signing in to your own accounts
when it opens a browser window, and clicking Allow when something asks permission.

## Which ones to connect

There's no fixed order — it depends on your business. `where-to-start.md` has the
list of what each one buys you, what it costs, and the four principles worth
knowing before you pick.

The short version: start where it hurts, but do one cheap one first to learn the
process, and remember nothing interesting happens until two systems are connected.

This file is about *how* to connect something. That one is about which ones and
why.

## What to expect

**Some connect in two minutes.** There's an official integration, you click
approve, done.

**Some take half an hour.** You'll end up on a developer settings page generating
a key. Claude will walk you through it. It looks intimidating and isn't.

**Some say no, or want you on a dearer plan.** That closes one door, not all of
them — Claude signs into the website as you and works the screens the way you
would. It's how plenty of useful things get done, and it's the right answer more
often than people expect.

Claude will tell you two things before it starts: some vendors' terms don't allow
it, and if one decides you look like a bot they can close the account your history
lives in. It'll also check the easy routes first — an export button, or just asking
the vendor for proper access, which usually nobody has. Then it's your call.

One hard stop: if the login belongs to head office or a franchisor, the risk lands
on them, so Claude won't use it. Two ways round that — ask them to grant you your
own login, or ask them to approve this and say so in writing. Both are usually a
short email.

Set aside a real block of time. Expect this one to be the annoying one.

## Rules worth having

**Never paste a password in.** If Claude needs you signed in, it opens
a browser window and you sign in there. It never needs to see the password itself.

**Keys and tokens go straight into settings, not into the conversation.** Claude
should move them for you. If you're ever asked to paste a long secret string into
the session, ask why first.

**Read-only first.** When something offers a choice, start with read access. Give
it the ability to change things once you trust what it's doing.

**Restart when you're ready to use it, not after every install.** Each new connector
needs the app fully quit and reopened before it becomes usable.

- **Connecting two or three in one sitting?** Do them all, then restart once at the
  end. Nothing is blocked while you wait — the browser still reads.
- **Spreading them over days?** Restart at the end of each sitting, before you walk
  away. A connector left unrestarted is a connector you can't use, and by next week
  you'll have forgotten why it isn't working and assume the connection failed.

**Never leave one hanging overnight.** The rule of thumb: don't finish for the day
with something connected but not restarted.

**Skills are different and need no restart** — they're live in the next session you
start. It's only connectors that need the app itself to come back up.

**Restart the app after connecting something.** Not just the chat window — fully
quit (Cmd+Q on a Mac) and reopen. New connections don't appear until you do.

## When it fails

It will sometimes. The useful move is to paste the error back in and say:

> "That didn't work. Here's exactly what it said. What's actually going wrong and
> what are our options?"

Don't go round in circles more than twice. If it's stuck twice, start a fresh session
and say what you already tried — a clean start fixes it more often than persisting.
