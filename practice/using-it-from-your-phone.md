# Using it from your phone

Most business owners aren't at a desk. You're on the floor, in the car, between
appointments — and that's exactly when you think of the question.

## What this gets you

Your computer does the work; your phone is the remote control. You ask from
wherever you are, it runs at home or at the office, and the answer comes back to
your phone.

That matters because everything useful lives on the computer — your connected
systems, your business folder, what Claude remembers. The phone on its own can't
reach any of it.

> *"What did we take yesterday?"* — from the car, at 7am, before you've opened a
> laptop.

## Setting it up

Ask Claude on your computer:

> "Set me up so I can send you jobs from my phone. Walk me through it."

It'll tell you what's needed and what to tap. Takes a few minutes and you do it
once.

## The one thing that catches people

**Your computer has to be awake.** A closed laptop is asleep, and a sleeping
computer does nothing.

Three ways round it, worst to best:

1. **Leave it open and plugged in** when you're out. Works, inelegant.
2. **Stop it sleeping while on power**, and let it sleep normally on battery. Ask
   Claude to set that up.
3. **An old machine left on somewhere.** The proper answer if you end up relying
   on this. A Mac mini or an old laptop in the office, always on, always reachable.

## What's worth doing from a phone, and what isn't

**Good from a phone**

- Asking about numbers you already have — yesterday's takings, this week against last
- Checking whether something ran — did the Monday report go out
- Kicking off a job you'll read later — *"pull the figures for the meeting at 2"*
- Capturing a thought before you lose it — *"remember to look at Thursday's drop"*

**Leave for the desk**

- Anything you need to read carefully before approving
- Connecting a new system — there's sign-in and clicking involved
- Building something for the first time
- Anything where you'd want to see the whole answer at once

## Is it private

The connection is between your phone and your own computer. Your business data
stays where it already is.

Normal phone sense still applies: your phone is now a way into your business, so
it wants a passcode on it like anything else would.
