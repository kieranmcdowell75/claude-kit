# List my systems

Two minutes. No connecting, no sign-ins, no decisions. Just find out what's there.

**Usually runs straight after `check-it-worked`, in the same session.** Don't send
someone to a fresh session for a two-minute job — do it here and finish it.

The point is that before this, Claude is guessing about their business. After it,
every conversation starts from what they actually run.

## Run through these out loud

Go category by category. One line each, and **"none" is a perfectly good answer.**

- **Email** — Gmail, Outlook, something else
- **Whatever runs the business day to day** — bookings, members, jobs, clients,
  patients. The thing staff are in all day
- **Customers and leads** — a CRM, a spreadsheet, or honestly nothing
- **Taking money** — the till, the payment system, the card machine
- **Accounting** — Xero, MYOB, QuickBooks
- **Team comms** — Slack, Teams, a WhatsApp group
- **Rostering and timesheets** — if it's separate from the above
- **Advertising** — Meta, Google, anything they pay for
- **Anything else they're in every week**

**Get the exact product name.** "Our booking system" is no use in three months.
"Lightspeed Restaurant" is. If they're not sure of the exact one, that's fine —
note what they said and move on, it can be pinned down when you connect it.

Don't ask how long they've had it, what plan they're on, or who set it up. Not
yet. That's the connection conversation.

## Then write it down

Put it in their business folder's `CLAUDE.md`, under the systems heading. A simple
table — the system, and what it holds. Nothing else.

Save it to memory too.

**Don't ask which system holds the "true" money figures, and don't record an
answer if they offer one.** It sounds like a sensible question and it is a trap.
The till, the booking system and the accounting package don't disagree because
one of them is wrong — they measure **different things**. The till holds what was
actually taken. The booking system holds what was billed or scheduled. Accounting
holds what's been reconciled, which commonly runs weeks or months behind, so the
recent months read low in it and nowhere else.

Write "Xero is the source of truth" into someone's house rules and you have
taught every future session to overrule the till with a figure that isn't
complete yet. **A disagreement between two systems is information, not an error to
settle by precedence.** Which number is right depends on the question being asked,
and that gets worked out when a report is built — with the owner, against a period
they already know.

## Then tell them the shape of what's ahead

For each one, a single line: roughly how long it'll take, whether there's likely
a ready-made connection, and whether it costs anything. Check
`connect-anything/connector-index.md` — several are already in there.

Flag the one that'll be hardest, and flag anything with a monthly fee.

**Don't rank them.** It's their business and they know where it hurts.

## Then hand over one next step

> *"That's the picture. Now pick the one you most want working, start a new session,
> and say: help me connect my [name]. One system per session — it keeps things clean
> and you get something finished each time."*

**One system per session matters.** A connection that goes badly doesn't drag the
others down with it, the conversation stays short enough to think in, and each one
ends with something done rather than a list of things half-started.

Don't connect anything in this session. This one is just the map.
