# Run a promotion

A promotion fails in the gaps. The wrong people get the text, a team member
sends the wrong version, one day's steps go out on the wrong day. Writing the
offer is the easy part. This skill is about the parts that make it land.

## Get the plan down first

Ask, and write the answers into one file, a campaign record, before building
anything:

1. **What's the offer, and who is it for?** New people, current customers, or
   both? Each has its own rules, set out under *Who it's for* below. A sale
   aimed at both needs two offers, so customers never see a deal that undercuts
   what they already pay.
2. **Which days does something go out?** Fewer send days means less for staff
   to get wrong. Each one has to earn its place.
3. **Who on the team sends, and who answers replies?**
4. **What counts as a sale, and where does it show?** Name the system that
   records it. That's where the results will come from.

Every ruling the owner makes goes into that record with the date: a price, a
wording, a group left out on purpose. In six months nobody remembers why a
group was excluded, and the record is the only thing that will.

## One source of truth

Keep the dates, the lists and the exact wording of every message in **one
place**. Build everything else from it: the team's instructions, the reminders,
the daily update. When one changes, rebuild the rest. Two copies of a message
always drift, and staff send the wrong one.

## Who it's for: three shapes

Settle which one this is before building a single list.

**New and lapsed people only.** Past buyers, enquiries that never bought,
people who used to be customers. Leave current customers out, and check again
just before sending: count how many current customers are on the list, and the
answer should be zero.

**Current customers only.** An add-on, an upgrade, a bring-a-friend offer, an
early renewal, a thank-you. These people already pay you, so the rules are
tighter:

- **The offer adds to what they have.** It must never be cheaper than what they
  already pay for the same thing, or the promotion becomes a reason to downgrade.
- **Split by what they hold.** Someone on the smallest plan and someone on the
  biggest need different messages. One generic text to all customers reads as
  junk mail from a business they trust.
- **Say what happens if they leave.** If the offer only works while they stay a
  customer, put that in the message and in the reply, in plain words.
- **Leave out people already on their way out.** Anyone who has given notice or
  is about to finish gets no upsell.
- **Expect more replies, and warmer ones.** Customers answer as if they're
  talking to a person, so someone has to answer within the day.

**Both, in the same sale.** Two lists, two offers, and **no one on both**.
Customers never receive the public deal. Check the two lists against each
other before either goes out, and give each its own day where you can.

## The lists

**Check the list against the system that actually knows.** The texting or email
tool usually holds a copy of who's a customer, and the copy lags. Compare the
two by email or phone number, never by name. Anyone the tool still thinks is a
customer but the real system doesn't, tag them out. Anyone in the real system
but missing from the tool usually can't be reached (no number, or they opted
out). Say so and move on.

**Perks look like customers.** Staff, family and complimentary plans pass most
"is a customer" filters. Leave them out by name.

**A count is the check.** Write the expected count beside each list. On the day,
if the number has jumped or dropped a lot, stop. Something changed.

## The messages

- The owner approves every word. Show the draft in the chat, not in a file.
- Load each message into the sending tool **as a draft, switched off**. A person
  switches it on, on the day, after sending themselves a test.
- If a message depends on something else being live first, such as a discount
  code or a web page, test that thing before the message goes. If the test
  fails, the message doesn't go.
- Opt-out wording and anything that sounds like a claim are the owner's call.
  Flag it plainly once, then follow their decision.

## What the team gets

- **One page for the whole sale:** the offers, the dates, what to say to each
  kind of reply. Read once beforehand.
- **One short instruction for each send day:** just that day's steps, in order,
  with a screenshot of each click. Nothing else on it.
- **A heads-up the working day before** each send, never on a weekend.
- **No clock times** unless one is truly fixed, such as "not before the code
  works". Staff follow times literally, and times go stale.

Send each day's instruction where the team already looks, at the same time each
morning.

## During the sale

- **A morning update:** what went out yesterday, replies, opt-outs, and sales
  from the system that records them. If sales couldn't be read, say that. Never
  show a zero.
- **Tag buyers from the sales system yourself.** Don't rely on staff to do it.
  Those tags keep buyers out of the next promotion.
- **A zero-dollar sale isn't free.** It can mean a giveaway, or money taken
  another way. List those for a person to check.
- **Before any send to current customers, re-check the list** against the real
  system. People leave mid-sale.

## How to check it's ready

- Every list's count matches the sending tool's own screen. Public lists have
  zero current customers, customer lists have only current ones, and nobody is
  on both.
- Every message reads exactly as approved, and every one is still switched off.
- Do a dry run of every day from the first heads-up to the day after the last
  send. Each day should print exactly what's expected, with nothing on weekends
  and nothing twice.

## Traps

- **Saving a list isn't always saving.** Some tools apply a filter to the screen
  without saving it. Reload and check the count again.
- **AND versus OR.** Adding a filter in the wrong place widens the list instead
  of narrowing it, and the tool gives no warning.
- **You can't filter on a tag that doesn't exist yet.** If buyers are tagged
  mid-sale, catch them in the re-check instead.
- **Moving a send day moves more than the message.** It moves the heads-up, the
  re-check, the day's instructions, and any "valid until" date. Change it in the
  one source and rebuild everything.
- **Shared pages can turn private.** Check a link opens for someone else before
  it goes to the team.

## What this can't tell you

- **Who read a message.** You get sent, clicked and replied, not read.
- **Sales taken outside the system** you're reading, such as cash or a second till.
- **Whether a code works for a particular customer.** Only a real test on the
  shop proves it.

## Never

Switch a message on, send one, or enrol anyone on the owner's behalf. Post
anywhere but the team's agreed channel. Put customers' names in anything that
leaves the business.

## Afterwards

Write down what sold, which list bought, and which day did the work, while it's
fresh. That's what the next sale gets planned from. Then `make-a-skill` to keep
this business's version.
